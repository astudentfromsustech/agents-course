## Answer

The state of the art is uneven: robust **data-to-contact-map inversion** exists for effective polymer models, whereas fully mechanistic inference of loop-extrusion kinetics or molecular LLPS force fields from bulk Hi-C/Micro-C remains limited. Maximum-entropy approaches (MiChroM/OpenMiChroM, GEM/IBD, PHi-C, and recent FI-Chrom) iteratively infer effective pairwise interaction parameters so that ensemble contact probabilities reproduce the input map; these are genuine inverse models, but their fitted potentials are not uniquely identifiable molecular mechanisms. [OpenMiChroM](https://open-michrom.readthedocs.io/en/latest/OpenMiChroM.html) [FI-Chrom](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12846836/) [PHi-C](https://academic.oup.com/nargab/article/2/2/lqaa020/5813971)

Mechanistic loop-extrusion models are usually calibrated by parameter sweeps, but there are now direct optimization examples: the original extrusion work searched 6,912 parameter combinations, and a newer loop-extrusion-plus-domain-attraction model uses Nelder–Mead to fit boundary permeability and attraction energies against Hi-C. [Goloborodko et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4889513/) [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.94738.2) SBS/PRISMR similarly infers a minimal arrangement of binding-site classes from a contact map, then forward-simulates it. [PRISMR/SBS application](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/6d1e911a-1263-45c1-bb68-d8b110a5b4b8/s41592-021-01135-1.pdf)

For an agent, treat this as a stochastic inverse problem: define a normalized contact observation model; fit a low-dimensional mechanistic model or high-dimensional effective potential; use replicate simulations; and score held-out distances/features, not only the training map. Pearson correlation alone is inadequate because shared distance decay can make unrelated maps correlate highly; SCC/HiCRep is a stronger global map score, but it still is not a mechanistic validation. [HiCRep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5668950/) The defensible standard is a panel: distance-stratified map similarity, \(P(s)\), insulation/TAD boundaries, compartment eigenvectors, and loop/peak recovery—ideally on held-out chromosomes, resolutions, perturbations, or imaging data. The major open problem is coupling protein-resolution condensate physics to chromosome-scale Hi-C inversion: published models couple *phenomenological* phase separation with extrusion, not CALVADOS/Mpipi-level condensates fitted to genome-wide Hi-C. [Combined LE+phase-separation model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9279381/)

## Key findings

- **Loop extrusion / Polychrom:** Polychrom is an OpenMM-based forward simulator intended for mechanistic hypotheses, especially loop extrusion, rather than reconstructing a polymer solely from a Hi-C map. Its usual inputs are polymer mechanics plus a 1D extrusion process (LEF loading, residence/processivity, separation, barrier positions/orientation/permeability); the original quantitative study grid-searched LEF processivity and separation and compared simulated and experimental contact scaling/maps. [Polychrom code](https://github.com/open2c/polychrom) [quantitative extrusion fit](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4889513/)

- **Actual loop-extrusion fitting is still low-dimensional.** The 2024 eLife model combines stochastic extrusion and domain attractions, simulates replicate maps, and uses Nelder–Mead to maximize Spearman correlation with Hi-C over directional boundary-stalling probabilities and domain attraction energies; it reports convergence after typically 70–90 iterations and validates against synthetic ground truth, perturbation maps, and FISH. [method and results](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.94738.2)

- **MiChroM / OpenMiChroM:** MiChroM is a coarse-grained chromosome energy model; OpenMiChroM exposes maximum-entropy-style training classes that accept a dense Hi-C matrix and optimize selected pairwise interaction/Lagrange-multiplier parameters by iterative simulation and Adam updates. The contact-kernel parameters and polymer/coarse-graining choices remain modelling assumptions. [OpenMiChroM optimisation API](https://open-michrom.readthedocs.io/en/latest/OpenMiChroM.html) [full-inversion tutorial](https://open-michrom.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Tutorials/Tutorial_Full_Inversion_Optimization.html)

- **FI-Chrom is the clearest recent full-map inversion.** It fits effective pair interactions for all locus pairs directly from Hi-C via GPU Langevin simulation and iterative gradient/Adam updates, explicitly describing tens of millions of fitted parameters at 50-kb bead resolution. This achieves close map reproduction but should be interpreted as an effective energy landscape, not identification of unique molecular interactions. [FI-Chrom](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12846836/)

- **Other analytic/effective inverse models are useful baselines.** PHi-C optimizes a polymer-network interaction matrix from Hi-C using an analytic relation and validates the reconstructed map with Pearson correlation before simulating dynamics. Gaussian Effective Model/IBD fits harmonic couplings to contact probabilities and has an exact equilibrium contact expression. [PHi-C](https://academic.oup.com/nargab/article/2/2/lqaa020/5813971) [GEM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6301988/)

- **SBS / PRISMR:** SBS represents chromatin as a polymer with cognate diffusing binders; PRISMR takes a contact map and uses simulated-annealing Monte Carlo to infer the minimal number and genomic arrangement of binding-site “colours” needed to explain it. Binding affinity/concentration and mapping from inferred colours to specific factors remain non-unique. [SBS model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3810333/) [PRISMR implementation description](https://www.eneagrid.enea.it/CRESCOreports/Report_CRESCO_2018.pdf)

- **HiP-HoP is principally predictive/data-informed, not a general Hi-C parameter-inference engine.** It uses 1D regulatory/epigenomic information, CTCF/cohesin-like loop extrusion, and bridging interactions to simulate loci; the available primary description says its adjustable interaction parameters can be derived from Hi-C, but broad automated likelihood-based fitting was not verified here. [HiP-HoP paper](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1007024) [unverified for a maintained automated fitting implementation]

- **Chrom3D is restraint-based structural inference, not force-field calibration.** It turns statistically significant TAD–TAD Hi-C interactions and optionally lamin-ChIP LADs into spatial/peripheral constraints, then samples bead-chain configurations by Monte Carlo. [Chrom3D paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5278575/) [code](https://github.com/Chrom3D/Chrom3D)

- **PASTIS, miniMDS, and 3DMax infer coordinates rather than a generative polymer mechanism.** PASTIS fits consensus structures with Poisson-based likelihood models; miniMDS converts normalized contact frequencies to assumed distances and uses partitioned MDS; 3DMax maximizes a likelihood while re-estimating the contact-frequency-to-distance exponent. They are useful initialization/structural baselines, but “fit” means positions and/or a conversion exponent, not extrusion or LLPS parameters. [PASTIS code](https://github.com/hiclib/pastis) [miniMDS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5870652/) [3DMax](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5824572/)

- **Bayesian treatment is real but mostly structure/ensemble inference, not modern genome-scale SBI.** ISD-style methods infer multistate polymer conformations and nuisance parameters with a Poisson observation model; yeast whole-nucleus work used MCMC to obtain posterior distributions over physical parameters from imaging and Hi-C-related observations. [population-contact Bayesian ensemble inference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7148566/) [yeast Bayesian physical inference](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1199-x)

- **ABC, neural posterior estimation, differentiable polymer dynamics, and learned emulator-based inference from bulk Hi-C were not found as established, maintained chromatin-polymer workflows in the primary sources reviewed.** The strongest identified ML acceleration is a graph neural network predicting thermodynamic polymer parameters from Hi-C, motivated by the expense of iterative maximum-entropy fitting; availability of production code was not verified. [AI + polymer model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12005555/) [unverified for public code]

- **CALVADOS, Mpipi, and OpenABC are molecular condensate engines, not Hi-C inverse models.** CALVADOS is an implicit-solvent, residue-level OpenMM framework parameterized for IDP phase behaviour; OpenABC provides GPU OpenMM implementations of HPS, MOFF, MRG-CG DNA, and Mpipi-related support. Their published targets are molecular/condensate properties, not chromosome-scale Hi-C map fitting. [CALVADOS code](https://github.com/KULL-Centre/CALVADOS) [OpenABC paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10153273/) [OpenABC code](https://github.com/ZhangGroup-MITChemistry/OpenABC) [Mpipi-T code](https://github.com/josephresearch/Mpipi-T)

- **Published coupling exists at phenomenological polymer resolution.** Combined extrusion + SBS/phase-separation simulations fit Hi-C and multiplexed-FISH at selected human loci, and the published code is available; this is not a calibrated CALVADOS/Mpipi-to-whole-genome Hi-C bridge. [combined-model paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9279381/) [simulation code](https://github.com/ehsanirani/PhaseSeparation-LoopExtrusion-MD)

- **Goodness-of-fit should not be one correlation.** HiCRep’s SCC smooths then computes a distance-stratified weighted correlation; its rationale is that ordinary Pearson can be spuriously high between distinct cell types because of shared genomic-distance decay. [HiCRep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5668950/)

- **Recommended acceptance panel:** report SCC on held-out map entries/distance strata; error for \(P(s)\); insulation-profile/boundary agreement; compartment-eigenvector correlation; loop/stripe/peak precision–recall at a declared caller and threshold; and orthogonal distance distributions or imaging where available. This is a methodological recommendation; no community source establishing a single mandatory composite criterion was found. [unverified for a field-wide standard]

- **The mechanism debate is not “loop extrusion versus LLPS” in a winner-take-all sense.** Cohesin/CTCF perturbations and extrusion simulations strongly support extrusion as a source of loops/TAD patterns, while compartmental segregation persists—and can strengthen—after cohesin loss; polymer models show extrusion can antagonize block-polymer segregation. [two-mechanism synthesis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6800258/) [extrusion–segregation simulations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6055145/)

- **The contested point is whether compartment-like segregation demonstrates intracellular LLPS.** Polymer microphase separation/bridging can generate compartment patterns without proving a bulk liquid droplet, and nuclear-condensate reviews explicitly note that many early in-vivo LLPS claims required stronger operational evidence and clearer definitions. [mechanistic distinction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11190842/) [critical review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6803379/)

- **Cost is dominated by resolution, equilibration, ensemble size, and optimization repetitions.** A polymer-based single-cell reconstruction reports a few GPU-hours at 20 kb/bead and days at 5 kb/bead; miniMDS reports kilobase-resolution whole-human structural inference in hours on a desktop, but it is MDS rather than forward MD. [GPU reconstruction cost](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10750380/) [miniMDS cost](https://github.com/seqcode/miniMDS)

- **Whole-genome forward polymer modelling is already large at coarse resolution.** A human genome at 50 kb/bead is estimated at >150,000 beads, while an inversion adds many independent simulation evaluations; full-map optimization should therefore be scheduled as multi-GPU batches and start at chromosome/region scale. [whole-genome bead estimate](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022283620306185) [FI-Chrom GPU inversion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12846836/)

## Caveats and open questions

- Bulk Hi-C/Micro-C is an ensemble, assay-dependent contact measurement; multiple parameter settings and mechanisms can yield similar maps. A high fitted correlation therefore does not establish causal molecular interpretation. Bayesian posterior uncertainty, identifiability tests, and perturbation/imaging validation are substantially underused. [Bayesian ensemble study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7148566/)

- I did not verify a mature published workflow that performs likelihood-based ABC/SBI or end-to-end differentiable MD inference of loop-extrusion/LLPS parameters from genome-wide Hi-C. Evidence for this particular frontier is thin; avoid claiming that it is established. [unverified]

- I did not verify a published, calibrated coupling in which CALVADOS, Mpipi, or OpenABC protein-condensate parameters are jointly inferred with chromosome-scale polymer parameters from Hi-C/Micro-C. Existing coupled work uses coarse-grained effective attractions/SBS-style binders instead. [CALVADOS](https://github.com/KULL-Centre/CALVADOS) [combined chromosome model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9279381/)

- “Phase separation” needs mechanism-specific language: polymer microphase separation, bridging-induced attraction, and protein LLPS can produce related spatial signatures but are not interchangeable claims. [critical discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6803379/) [mechanistic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11190842/)

- No universal simulation-to-Hi-C score or threshold was verified. SCC is rigorous for map reproducibility, but a mechanistic model should be judged by predeclared multi-scale and out-of-sample predictions rather than its optimized training score alone. [HiCRep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5668950/)
